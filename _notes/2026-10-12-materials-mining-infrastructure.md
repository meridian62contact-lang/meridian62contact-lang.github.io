### What happened

Heidelberg Materials has agreed to buy NCC's <a class="glossary-term" href="/glossary/#term-aggregates">aggregates</a> and asphalt businesses in Sweden and Norway for about SEK 5.5bn [1]. It is Heidelberg's part of NCC's SEK 8.2bn sale of its Industry division, with CRH buying the Danish and Finnish operations [2]. The deal includes 106 aggregates sites and 44 <a class="glossary-term" href="/glossary/#term-hot-mix-asphalt">hot-mix asphalt</a> plants. Heidelberg says the acquisition strengthens its exposure to infrastructure spending — the obvious reading is that the company wants more capacity. The more important point is where the new capacity sits.

### What people are missing

Aggregates are heavy and relatively cheap, so transport quickly becomes a major part of the <a class="glossary-term" href="/glossary/#term-delivered-cost">delivered cost</a>. A quarry close to a road project has a clear advantage over one much farther away, even if both produce the same material at the same factory cost. UK competition authorities have often treated aggregates as local markets, with around 30 miles used as a typical starting point for a quarry's <a class="glossary-term" href="/glossary/#term-catchment-area">catchment area</a> [3]. The quarries also come with <a class="glossary-term" href="/glossary/#term-permit-quarry-licence">permits</a>, which are slow to get — Heidelberg knows this from its own limestone quarry at Slite on Gotland, where a long-term permit was refused in 2021 and a 30-year licence only came through in 2025 [4].

Asphalt is even more local. Hot-mix asphalt has to be <a class="glossary-term" href="/glossary/#term-compaction">compacted</a> before it cools to about 80°C [5], and it cools faster in cold air and wind [6], so the distance a plant can serve shrinks in a Nordic spring or autumn. European Commission merger analysis has treated asphalt markets as highly local, with around 40km used as a service radius in some cases [7]. That means Heidelberg is buying 150 extra production points, not simply more tonnes of output. A denser network should cut delivery distances and put more infrastructure projects within reach of a Heidelberg site.

### Why it matters

If Heidelberg has a site 15km from a project while a rival is 70km away, the rival starts with a transport-cost disadvantage. So Heidelberg is not only betting on stronger Nordic infrastructure demand — it is making sure more of that demand sits close to one of its own sites. The main risk is <a class="glossary-term" href="/glossary/#term-warm-mix-asphalt">warm-mix asphalt</a>, which can be compacted at lower temperatures [6]. If it becomes standard, asphalt plants could serve a wider area and the advantage of being close would shrink.

### What we are watching next

<a class="glossary-term" href="/glossary/#term-regulatory-approval">Regulatory approval</a> comes next, and NCC expects the deal to complete in the second half of 2027 [2]. Regulators are likely to focus on areas where Heidelberg and NCC sites serve the same catchment. Any forced quarry or asphalt-plant sales would support the idea that location is the key source of value in the deal.
