### What happened

In October 2026, investors demanded a <a class="glossary-term" href="/glossary/#term-yield">yield</a> of more than 15% to hold the $1.25bn of 9.625% <a class="glossary-term" href="/glossary/#term-unsecured">unsecured</a> <a class="glossary-term" href="/glossary/#term-notes">notes</a> that CoreWeave (NASDAQ: CRWV), an <a class="glossary-term" href="/glossary/#term-ai-cloud-provider">AI cloud provider</a>, issued in June 2026 [1][2], and its share price fell about 30% between its 30 September 2026 and 29 October 2026 closes [3]. The concern is the cost of its expansion: CoreWeave had about $35.6bn of debt at 30 June 2026, and its second-quarter <a class="glossary-term" href="/glossary/#term-net-interest-expense">net interest expense</a> rose to $640m from $267m a year earlier, against <a class="glossary-term" href="/glossary/#term-adjusted-operating-income">adjusted operating income</a> of only $128m [2][4]. For the third quarter of 2026, the company has <a class="glossary-term" href="/glossary/#term-guidance">guided</a> to $860m–$940m of interest expense and $200m–$260m of adjusted operating income [5].

### What people are missing

The figures suggest that the <a class="glossary-term" href="/glossary/#term-binding-limit">binding limit</a> is the <a class="glossary-term" href="/glossary/#term-plant">plant</a> rather than demand: at 30 June 2026 CoreWeave had a <a class="glossary-term" href="/glossary/#term-revenue-backlog">revenue backlog</a> of about $104bn but only about 1.5 <a class="glossary-term" href="/glossary/#term-gw-gigawatt">GW</a> of <a class="glossary-term" href="/glossary/#term-active-power">active power</a> [4], against about 4.2 GW of <a class="glossary-term" href="/glossary/#term-contracted-power">contracted power</a> by 11 August 2026 [6], so less than half of the power it had secured was running. That plant is paid for up front, with $14.1bn spent on <a class="glossary-term" href="/glossary/#term-property-and-equipment">property and equipment</a> in the first half of 2026 alone [4], yet a site earns nothing until its power, chips, networking, and cooling are installed and <a class="glossary-term" href="/glossary/#term-commissioning">commissioned</a>. Liquids absorb much more heat than air, so many CoreWeave data centres use <a class="glossary-term" href="/glossary/#term-closed-loop-liquid-cooling">closed-loop liquid cooling</a>, including <a class="glossary-term" href="/glossary/#term-direct-to-chip">direct-to-chip</a> systems that pass coolant through plates attached to the processors [7], and good practice when commissioning such a loop is to flush out <a class="glossary-term" href="/glossary/#term-fabrication-debris">fabrication debris</a>, then test the circulating coolant for <a class="glossary-term" href="/glossary/#term-glycol">glycol</a> concentration, <a class="glossary-term" href="/glossary/#term-ph">pH</a>, <a class="glossary-term" href="/glossary/#term-reserve-alkalinity">reserve alkalinity</a> and <a class="glossary-term" href="/glossary/#term-corrosion-inhibitor">corrosion-inhibitor</a> levels, with <a class="glossary-term" href="/glossary/#term-dissolved-metals">dissolved metals</a> an early warning of <a class="glossary-term" href="/glossary/#term-corrosion">corrosion</a> [8]. We have found no evidence that cooling has delayed CoreWeave's projects — the point is that every new site must pass through a physical commissioning sequence before it can earn, while interest is charged on borrowed money from the day it is drawn. Even CoreWeave's target of more than 1.85 GW of active power by the end of 2026 would be less than half of the 4.2 GW contracted [5][6].

### Why it matters

In plain terms, in the second quarter of 2026 CoreWeave paid its lenders five times what its day-to-day business earned, and its own forecasts imply that the gap will widen in the third quarter [4][5]. At the same time borrowing is getting more complicated: US consumer prices rose 3.7% in the year to September 2026 [9], and the US central bank raised interest rates in September 2026, then kept them at 3.75%–4% on 28 October 2026, with two officials voting for a further rise [10][11]. Customers have already signed up for about $104bn of future work [4], so the question is not whether people want CoreWeave's computing power but whether it can switch on data centres fast enough to keep up with its interest bill.

### What we are watching next

CoreWeave holds its third-quarter results call on 10 November 2026, the same day the US publishes its October inflation data [12][9]. We will compare net interest expense with the $860m–$940m guidance, and active power with the target of more than 1.85 GW by the end of 2026 [5]. Active power rising faster than interest costs would challenge our reading, while interest climbing as capacity lags would confirm it.
