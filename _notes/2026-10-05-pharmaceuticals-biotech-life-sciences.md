### What happened

Novo Nordisk (NOVO-B) reported topline Phase 3 results for CagriSema, its investigational once-weekly treatment for weight management and type 2 diabetes that combines cagrilintide and semaglutide, on 21 September 2026 [1]. In REIMAGINE 5, CagriSema delivered an estimated average weight loss of 12.4% at week 60 versus 9.1% with Eli Lilly's tirzepatide 5 mg in adults with type 2 diabetes, with a <a class="glossary-term" href="/glossary/#term-non-inferior-hba1c-reduction">non-inferior HbA1c reduction</a> of 1.71% versus 1.67% [1]. REDEFINE 9, which focused on obese or overweight adults, reported 21.0% weight reduction at week 68 with CagriSema versus 2.0% with placebo [1].

### What people are missing

REIMAGINE 5 is the first trial in which CagriSema outperformed tirzepatide on weight loss, after it failed to show non-inferiority to Eli Lilly's drug in REDEFINE 4 (23% vs 25.5%) [2]. REIMAGINE 5 and REDEFINE 9 tested CagriSema at 1.0 mg/1.0 mg, less than half the dose that previously fell short against tirzepatide 15 mg (2.4 mg/2.4 mg) [1,2]. At its Capital Markets Day, Novo framed the two low-dose CagriSema results around <a class="glossary-term" href="/glossary/#term-tolerability">tolerability</a> and dose flexibility, noting that not all patients receive or remain on the highest dose of a therapy [1]. However, both headline weight-loss figures were calculated with the efficacy estimand, which assumes every trial participant continues treatment and so does not account for how many patients can actually stay on the drug [3]. The efficacy estimand tends to yield higher numbers than the treatment-regimen estimand, which counts everyone regardless of adherence: in REDEFINE 4, including the entire population reduced CagriSema's estimated average weight loss by 2.8 percentage points, and tirzepatide's by 1.9 percentage points [2]. The results are a clear efficacy win for Novo, but the case it is making for tolerability will only be tested once the full trial data is released.

### Why it matters

CagriSema's potential launch highlights a pivotal shift in the cardiometabolic therapeutics industry, as the market moves from a race for regulatory approval to a race to differentiate. The focus is no longer simply greater weight loss, but dose efficiency, tolerability and the convenience of a once-weekly injection. A lower dose matters clinically because nausea — a significant reason for treatment discontinuation — rises with dose, making the balance between efficacy and tolerability increasingly important. Commercially, greater dose efficiency enables higher patient volume per unit of active pharmaceutical ingredient (API) production capacity. In a global obesity medicine market projected at $92bn in 2026 [4], this could expand patient access while improving <a class="glossary-term" href="/glossary/#term-manufacturing-economics">manufacturing economics</a> and helping protect margins.

### What we are watching next

Novo and Lilly are now pursuing different routes to growth. Lilly is expanding GLP-1 access into low- and middle-income countries, where an estimated seven in ten adults with obesity live [5], while Novo is diversifying its pipeline, targeting at least five late-stage drugs outside obesity and diabetes by 2030 [6]. With an FDA decision expected this quarter, CagriSema could open another path by moving the obesity market toward personalised treatment, tested around several dose strengths adjusted for factors such as weight, diabetes status and tolerability.
