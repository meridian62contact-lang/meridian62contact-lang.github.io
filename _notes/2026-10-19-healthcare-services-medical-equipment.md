### What happened

On 5 October 2026, GE HealthCare (GEHC) agreed to acquire SOFIE Biosciences for $945 million in cash [1]. SOFIE makes the radioactive <a class="glossary-term" href="/glossary/#term-tracer">tracers</a> that patients are injected with before a <a class="glossary-term" href="/glossary/#term-pet-scan">PET scan</a>. It runs 15 production sites with 21 <a class="glossary-term" href="/glossary/#term-cyclotron">cyclotrons</a> across the US and already manufactures GE HealthCare's heart-imaging tracer, Flyrcado [1]. The deal also includes US rights to FAPI-74, a <a class="glossary-term" href="/glossary/#term-late-stage-phase-3">late-stage</a> tracer being tested for imaging many types of cancer, and is expected to close in the first half of 2027 [1]. The company expects the acquisition to add to its revenue growth, <a class="glossary-term" href="/glossary/#term-adjusted-ebit-margin">adjusted EBIT margin</a>, and <a class="glossary-term" href="/glossary/#term-adjusted-earnings-per-share">adjusted earnings per share</a> in its first full year of ownership [1].

### What people are missing

On the surface, this is a company buying one of its suppliers. The deeper logic is physics. Tracers such as Flyrcado are made with <a class="glossary-term" href="/glossary/#term-fluorine-18">fluorine-18</a>, a radioactive atom with a <a class="glossary-term" href="/glossary/#term-half-life">half-life</a> of 110 minutes [1]. In practice, a dose made at 8am has lost more than half its strength by 10am, and almost 80% by midday (our calculation). A tracer cannot be stored or shipped long distances: it has to be made close to the hospital and used the same day. Fluorine-18 sits in a useful middle ground. It lasts much longer than the other tracers commonly used for heart PET scans, which is what allows ready-made doses to be delivered from a regional site [2], but not long enough to travel far. Without a tracer on time, a PET scanner cannot be used, so supply directly affects how hospitals use imaging equipment. This suggests GE HealthCare is paying mainly for location: a network of 15 sites and 21 cyclotrons near hospitals that would be hard to rebuild quickly [1].

### Why it matters

Tracers are a smaller but faster-growing part of GE HealthCare than its scanners. In the second quarter of 2026, its <a class="glossary-term" href="/glossary/#term-pharmaceutical-diagnostics-segment">Pharmaceutical Diagnostics</a> segment grew 14.6% on an <a class="glossary-term" href="/glossary/#term-organic-growth">organic</a> basis, compared with 5.0% for <a class="glossary-term" href="/glossary/#term-advanced-imaging-solutions">Advanced Imaging Solutions</a>, the segment that sells its imaging equipment [5]. Demand is also widening beyond the heart: GE HealthCare's Vizamyl, a fluorine-18 tracer that shows <a class="glossary-term" href="/glossary/#term-amyloid-plaques">amyloid plaques</a> in the brain, now has a US label covering the selection of patients for Alzheimer's treatments and checking whether plaques have cleared [3]. Each scan depends on a fresh dose arriving within hours, and with SOFIE, GE HealthCare would cover the chain from the cyclotron to the image [1].

### What we are watching next

GE HealthCare reports third-quarter results on 28 October 2026 [4]. We are watching whether Pharmaceutical Diagnostics, which SOFIE will join, keeps growing faster than the imaging equipment business [1][5].
