### What happened

On 18 November, Barclays reinforced its confidence in achieving its strategic targets at the J.P. Morgan UK Leaders Conference [1]. The bank reiterated its ambition to achieve a <a class="glossary-term" href="/glossary/#term-return-on-tangible-equity-rote">return on tangible equity (RoTE)</a> above 14% by 2028, alongside cumulative shareholder distributions of at least £15bn through 2028 [1]. This follows its third-quarter results, which revealed a 47% year-on-year increase in <a class="glossary-term" href="/glossary/#term-equities-trading-revenue">equities trading revenue</a> to £1.01bn, contrasting with a 19% decline in <a class="glossary-term" href="/glossary/#term-fixed-income-currencies-and-commodities-ficc">fixed-income, currencies and commodities (FICC)</a> revenue [2]. Despite this divergence, total Global Markets revenue increased 4% year-on-year [2].

### What people are missing

The contrasting performance of equities and FICC reveals more than differences in trading conditions. Barclays' management stated that approximately 90% of its equities revenue growth between Q3 YTD 2023 and Q3 YTD 2026 came from <a class="glossary-term" href="/glossary/#term-equity-derivatives">derivatives</a> and <a class="glossary-term" href="/glossary/#term-prime-financing">prime financing</a>, areas in which the bank has deliberately invested [1]. This suggests that the improvement is not solely the result of favourable market conditions but also reflects Barclays' strategic efforts to expand its equities franchise.

More importantly, Barclays' financing income, which tends to be more stable than trading-related intermediation income, grew 8% year-on-year, supported by prime financing. Meanwhile, FICC weakness was concentrated in intermediation activities [2].

This highlights the distinction between <a class="glossary-term" href="/glossary/#term-structural-revenue-growth">structural growth</a>, driven by long-term investments and client relationships, and <a class="glossary-term" href="/glossary/#term-cyclical-revenue">cyclical growth</a>, which depends on temporary market conditions. Barclays' expanding prime financing business could therefore provide a more consistent earnings base, even when fixed-income trading weakens. However, derivatives revenues remain sensitive to market activity, meaning that strong quarterly performance does not necessarily guarantee sustainable earnings growth.

### Why it matters

Barclays' FICC performance was affected by subdued interest-rate trading activity and challenging <a class="glossary-term" href="/glossary/#term-credit-intermediation">credit intermediation</a> conditions [3]. This highlights how trading opportunities can differ across asset classes, making revenue diversification increasingly important for investment banks.

More importantly, this has implications for Barclays' valuation. At the conference, a J.P. Morgan analyst highlighted that Barclays traded at approximately 0.8 times projected 2028 tangible book value, meaning investors valued the bank below its forecast tangible net assets. This suggests that the market may be questioning whether Barclays can generate returns sufficient to justify a higher valuation, despite the analyst's projected 2028 RoTE of 15.2%, above management's target of more than 14% [3].

If Barclays can demonstrate that its earnings growth is sustainable rather than dependent on favourable trading conditions, investors may become more confident in its ability to generate returns above its <a class="glossary-term" href="/glossary/#term-cost-of-equity">cost of equity</a>. This could support a higher valuation relative to tangible book value as the bank progresses towards its 2028 profitability target.

### What we are watching next

Management expects fourth-quarter trading conditions to be more challenging for FICC than for equities [3]. We are therefore watching whether continued growth in equity derivatives and prime financing can offset fixed-income weakness and sustain overall investment banking profitability. Barclays' upcoming full-year results and February guidance will also be important for assessing progress towards its above-14% RoTE ambition and £15bn shareholder distribution commitment. Evidence of more <a class="glossary-term" href="/glossary/#term-earnings-resilience">resilient earnings</a> would strengthen the case for a <a class="glossary-term" href="/glossary/#term-valuation-rerating">valuation rerating</a>, while persistent trading weakness could raise concerns about the sustainability of its profitability targets.
