### What happened

Lithium prices fell sharply in October. The most-traded lithium carbonate futures contract in China fell 11.8% in a single day on 12 October, to about US$15,640 per tonne — its lowest level since December 2025 and roughly half its peak on 13 May, amid growing worries about demand in 2027 [1]. Albemarle (NYSE: ALB), which produces lithium from <a class="glossary-term" href="/glossary/#term-brine">brine</a> at Chile's Salar de Atacama, reports third-quarter results on 4 November [2]. Its main project in Chile is a US$3.1bn plan to add <a class="glossary-term" href="/glossary/#term-direct-lithium-extraction-dle">direct lithium extraction (DLE)</a> to its existing operations, which is now under Chile's environmental review [3]. The obvious question is whether lower prices make that spending harder to justify.

### What people are missing

The answer depends on how much lithium the current process loses. Albemarle pumps brine into evaporation ponds and lets the sun remove the water, and the route from pumping to finished product can take up to 24 months [4]. As the water evaporates, other salts crystallise out and are removed, while the lithium concentration increases from about 0.2% to 6% [5]. <a class="glossary-term" href="/glossary/#term-solar-evaporation">Solar evaporation</a> recovers less than half of the lithium in the brine [4], so more than half of the lithium Albemarle pumps never reaches the customer. DLE works differently. The brine passes over a solid material, such as a manganese, titanium or aluminium oxide, that picks up <a class="glossary-term" href="/glossary/#term-lithium-ions">lithium ions</a> and leaves most of the other dissolved salts behind [4]. Albemarle's <a class="glossary-term" href="/glossary/#term-pilot-plant">pilot plant</a> recovered more than 94% of the lithium [6].

### Why it matters

Albemarle currently takes a <a class="glossary-term" href="/glossary/#term-net-brine-extraction">net</a> 442 litres of brine per second out of the salt flat (the amount pumped out minus the amount put back), and the DLE plan does not require an increase in the permitted extraction volume [3][6]. So the way to grow in Chile is to get more lithium out of the same brine. With the first DLE line, Albemarle expects to recover nearly twice as much lithium while cutting net pumping to 342 litres per second, and to as little as 142 with all six lines [3]. Getting more lithium from less pumping means each tonne costs less to produce. When prices fall, the producers with the lowest costs are the ones that keep making money, so lower prices make the case for DLE stronger, as long as Albemarle can afford to build it.

### What we are watching next

At the results on 4 November [2], we are watching for any change to the DLE timeline. Albemarle has already cut its 2026 <a class="glossary-term" href="/glossary/#term-capital-expenditure">capital spending</a> plan to about US$500m, down 15% on 2025 [7], and plans to start with one production line while it proves the technology at <a class="glossary-term" href="/glossary/#term-commercial-scale">commercial scale</a> [8]. A further delay would suggest that lower prices are slowing the project down.
