### What happened

GE Vernova's Grid segment reported another quarter of strong orders, pushing backlog to a new high and keeping book-to-bill above 1 [1]. The obvious reading is that demand remains exceptionally strong, but the size of the backlog is becoming less useful as a measure of how quickly the business can grow.

### What people are missing

The issue is increasingly physical capacity. Large power-transformer lead times have stretched from under a year to more than two years in some markets [2], while specialised electrical steel and skilled manufacturing labour remain difficult to expand quickly [3]. This means GE Vernova can continue winning orders without being able to turn them into revenue at the same pace. The more useful question is therefore not how large the backlog becomes, but whether newer orders are being won at prices that compensate for the scarcity of manufacturing capacity.

### Why it matters

In plain terms, customers may be willing to buy more transformers than GE Vernova can currently build. That is good for pricing power, but it also means a growing order book does not automatically translate into equally fast near-term revenue growth.

### What we are watching next

At the next results, watch the Grid segment margin rather than the backlog headline. Rising margins would suggest newer, higher-priced orders are beginning to replace older contracts, while another backlog increase without margin improvement would suggest that conversion remains the bottleneck.
