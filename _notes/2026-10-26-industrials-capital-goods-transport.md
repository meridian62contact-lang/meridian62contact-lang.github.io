### What happened

France's draft 2027 budget proposes raising a tax on the most profitable motorway and large-airport operators from 4.6% to as much as 12.2%, potentially generating an additional €800m annually across the operators affected [1]. The proposal comes as three of VINCI's historic French motorway <a class="glossary-term" href="/glossary/#term-concession">concessions</a> approach expiry: Escota in February 2032, Cofiroute in June 2034 and ASF in April 2036 [2]. In 2025, VINCI Autoroutes generated €2.64bn in <a class="glossary-term" href="/glossary/#term-free-cash-flow">free cash flow</a>, accounting for approximately 38% of the <a class="glossary-term" href="/glossary/#term-group">group</a>'s total [3].

### What people are missing

Placing emphasis on the 38% is crucial, as VINCI Autoroutes brought in only 9% of the group's revenue that same year [3]. This gap can be explained by the motorway being already built and cheap to run: the road has been paid for, drivers arrive without needing to be won as customers, and staff and maintenance costs are small compared with what is collected in <a class="glossary-term" href="/glossary/#term-toll">tolls</a>. In 2025, VINCI Autoroutes' <a class="glossary-term" href="/glossary/#term-ebitda">EBITDA</a> margin was 71%, compared with 9.5% at Energy Solutions, the group's largest business by revenue [3]. The motorways are therefore a relatively small part of what VINCI sells and a large part of what it keeps. That distinction changes what the expiry dates mean: judged by revenue, VINCI loses roughly a tenth of its sales when these concessions end, but judged by cash, it loses more than a third of what the entire group generates [3]. VINCI has reduced its reliance on these motorways for earnings: their share of group EBITDA fell from roughly 59% in 2006 to about 35% in 2025 [2]. That 35% is lower than the 38% free cash flow share because EBITDA is measured before investment, tax and interest. Even so, the businesses growing in their place are not there yet in cash terms: in 2025 VINCI Airports generated €1.25bn of free cash flow and Energy Solutions €1.20bn, each under half of Autoroutes' €2.64bn, while VINCI Highways, the overseas roads arm, generated €86m [3].

### Why it matters

The proposed tax increase could put pressure on VINCI's near-term earnings, while the expiry of its historic motorway concessions presents a longer-term challenge to cash flow generation. Although Airports, Energy Solutions and international highways are contributing to the group's diversification, their combined 2025 free cash flow of approximately €2.53bn remained below VINCI Autoroutes' €2.64bn [3]. For investors, the key question is whether these businesses can grow enough to replace the cash flows from the expiring concessions.

### What we are watching next

At VINCI's full-year results in February 2027, we will be watching whether the company states what the higher tax would cost, since its 2026 guidance was explicitly based on no change in taxation [3]. Over the longer run, the figure to follow is the combined free cash flow of Airports, Energy Solutions and Highways. In 2025, the three together generated €2.53bn (own calculation), still short of Autoroutes' €2.64bn despite earning several times its revenue [3].
