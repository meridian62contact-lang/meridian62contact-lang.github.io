### What happened

On 28 October 2026, Roche (ROG) launched VENTANA BAP1 (SP527), a tissue test that helps pathologists tell malignant mesothelioma apart from lung cancer, other cancers that have spread to the chest or abdomen, and benign tissue growth [1]. It is an automated immunohistochemistry stain and Roche says it is meant to be used alongside two stains it launched recently, VENTANA MTAP (SP530) and VENTANA Claudin-4 (EP417) [1]. The launch came six days after Roche's nine-month sales update, in which Pathology Lab was the fastest-growing part of its Diagnostics division, with sales up 10% at constant exchange rates compared with 4% for the division as a whole [2].

### What people are missing

A missing BAP1 signal is a reliable sign of cancer but a normal one proves very little. BAP1 is a tumour suppressor and when a stain shows the protein is missing from the cell nucleus, Roche says the result is virtually 100% specific for malignancy [1], and the meta-analysis puts pooled specificity at 1.00 [3]. Yet in a large meta-analysis of 12 studies and 1,824 patients, loss of BAP1 had a pooled sensitivity of just 56% [3], and it appeared in about 74% of epithelioid cases but only 7% of sarcomatoid ones [4], so a normal result cannot rule the cancer out. Pathologists therefore need a second marker that looks for a different genetic change, and loss of MTAP is used as a stand-in for the deletion of CDKN2A, a separate tumour suppressor gene [4]. Because biopsy tissue is limited, Roche presents BAP1 as a single, highly specific marker that leaves enough tissue for molecular testing, and states that the stain is intended for use alongside its MTAP and Claudin-4 tests [1].

### Why it matters

For patients, a clearer answer from a small sample matters in a cancer where most people are diagnosed late and median survival is 12 to 18 months [1]. Mesothelioma is rare, but the same question comes up on chest samples that may turn out to be lung cancer, which is diagnosed in about 2.6 million people a year and for which Roche already supplies several tissue tests [1][6]. Labs repeat these tests for every patient, and a panel of three stains consumes three Roche products where one was used before, in a tissue-testing business that has already grown twice as fast as its diagnostics unit overall in the first nine months of 2026 [2].

### What we are watching next

Roche reports full-year results in the last week of January, on 29 January 2026 for 2025 and 30 January 2025 for 2024; the 2026 date is not yet out [7]. Two numbers in it will show how fast labs are taking the panel up. One is whether Pathology Lab again grows faster than Diagnostics as a whole. The other is advanced staining, up 7% over the first nine months [2][5]. Uptake may be slow, because labs pick their own panels and one already running a BAP1 stain from another supplier has little reason to change. If advanced staining is still at 7% or below, and Pathology Lab's growth has come from instruments and routine tests, we have read this wrong.
