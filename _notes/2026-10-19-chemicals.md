### What happened

On 25 September, BASF confirmed that it recently made a merger proposal to Evonik and its biggest shareholder, the RAG-Stiftung foundation [1]. Sabine Waldeck reported that Evonik had rejected an initial €10.3bn offer, equivalent to €22.15 per share, as too low, although sources suggested BASF could consider a higher bid [2]. With BASF facing growing competition from China's chemical producers, the potential deal raises a bigger question: can acquiring Evonik strengthen BASF's competitive position, or will greater scale do little to resolve the cost pressures facing European chemicals?

### What people are missing

This acquisition comes at a time when BASF faces fierce competition from China and risks losing its lead in the chemical industry: the chemicals division of China's Sinopec now generates revenue comparable to BASF's €59.7bn [3]. This competition has also prompted BASF to invest €8.7bn in a new Verbund site in Zhanjiang, Guangdong Province, which opened on 26 March 2026 as the company's largest-ever investment and its third-largest site worldwide [4]. The proposed takeover may therefore be an attempt to <a class="glossary-term" href="/glossary/#term-consolidate-the-market">consolidate the market</a> and defend BASF's position in the face of rising Chinese competition.

### Why it matters

At €10.3bn [5], approximately a quarter of BASF's market value [6], this would be a major commitment. The timing is also important: BASF is currently <a class="glossary-term" href="/glossary/#term-restructuring">restructuring</a> and selling businesses to repair its finances [7], and still expects only around €1.5–2.3bn of <a class="glossary-term" href="/glossary/#term-free-cash-flow">free cash flow</a> in 2026 [8]. Financing is therefore key: BASF has not said how it would pay, but a cash offer of this size would most likely be funded largely with debt, putting pressure on BASF's <a class="glossary-term" href="/glossary/#term-balance-sheet">balance sheet</a> and potentially its dividend. In return, Evonik would increase BASF's exposure towards <a class="glossary-term" href="/glossary/#term-specialty-chemicals">specialty chemicals</a>, where earnings are less dependent on price swings than in <a class="glossary-term" href="/glossary/#term-commodity-chemicals">commodity chemical</a> businesses [9]. Even so, BASF shares fell 3.6% on the day it confirmed the talks [1][5], suggesting investors are not yet convinced the trade-off is worth it.

### What we are watching next

The key question is whether BASF returns with a revised offer. BASF's third-quarter results, scheduled for 28 October 2026 [10], should also provide a useful update on its <a class="glossary-term" href="/glossary/#term-operating-environment">operating environment</a> as we assess whether a larger group could address the competitive pressures facing European chemical producers [11]. Other factors to watch include the treatment of existing employment commitments, the potential structure of a transaction and any regulatory requirements [3]. With discussions still at an <a class="glossary-term" href="/glossary/#term-exploratory-talks">exploratory</a> stage, the next signal to watch is whether both sides can agree on a valuation that makes the deal viable.
